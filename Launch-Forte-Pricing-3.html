What counts as a system

A system is any app your business already uses that the build has to touch. Your CRM is one. Your accounting software is one. Your website forms, your phone system, your email, a spreadsheet you keep numbers in, each one is a system.

The tool doing the connecting does not count. Zapier, Make, or whatever runs the automation in the background is plumbing rather than a place your information lives, so you never pay a line for it.

Counting yours: a build that pulls website forms into your CRM and pushes paid invoices into QuickBooks touches three systems. The first two are already inside the prices above, so the third adds one line at $300.

Always inside the number, never billed on top. Testing on your real data rather than a sandbox, the handoff documentation so you own what was built, one walkthrough session, and thirty days of support after go live.

Why ten decisions and not one. "Work out what to reorder" sounds like a single feature. In a real warehouse it is ten separate rules, and each one is a place a system quietly gives you a wrong number if nobody built it.
  1. Deciding what one unit even is, when the same item is counted as singles and as cases
  2. Coverage measured against real demand rather than the calendar
  3. Every item measured against its own lead time
  4. Netting products that pull from one shared pool
  5. Rolling a case up through its parts to the limiting component
  6. Purchase orders past their date, flagged and held out of the count
  7. Healthy overall but short at one location, with a transfer suggested
  8. Missing or broken data flagged rather than quietly dropped
  9. Items with stock but no forecast, left neutral instead of guessed
  10. The reorder recommendation itself

Three Rules I Hold Myself To

The prices never move, only the count does

A bigger job is more pieces, never a higher rate. If your build turns out to have more in it than the job description said, you see the full list and the new number before any work starts, never after.

The number is fixed before I start

No hourly billing, no meter running, no invoice at the end that is larger than the number you agreed to. If the scope genuinely changes, that is a conversation before it happens rather than a line item after.

If I cannot price it honestly, I will not price it

Some jobs do not have enough detail to quote without guessing. When that happens I will tell you exactly which two or three things I need to know instead of putting a made up number in front of you.
