The longer version

I spent years doing work that looked right on paper and never quite felt right. Enterprise infrastructure, millions of transactions, the kind of environment where you learn to think three moves ahead because the system cannot afford a single misstep. I was good at it. I just wasn't lit up by it.

I think everyone has a thing they were built to do. Nothing mystical about it. There's a version of your day that fits, and when you get close to it, you feel the difference.

So I started asking a sharper question. Forget what reads well on a resume or sounds impressive across a dinner table. What did I really want, and what made me come alive?

It turned out to be this. Taking something held together by hand, a CRM nobody ever configured, a process that lives only in one person's head, and rebuilding it into something that runs on its own and keeps running long after I've handed it over.

I started Launch Forte on the side. Nights and weekends, in whatever hours I could find. No plan and no network, just the work. Seven years later, sixty plus systems built across twelve industries. Still learning. Still building.

What I've figured out so far

Everything is a system if you look at it right. A business, a process, a person, a market. Once you can see the shape of it, you can see where it bends and where it breaks.

Most problems aren't as complicated as they look. Most complexity is just confusion that never got untangled.

I would rather do a few things well than many things badly.

And I care whether the thing I build still works six months from now, when the traffic has doubled and the team that hired me is long gone. Most people in this work don't stick around to find out. I do.

If you want to work together

I run Launch Forte. I build the systems a company runs on once it outgrows doing things by hand, and I architect them to hold when the weight comes. Fixed scope and a fixed price, built to last.

I like the messy ones, where the problem is hiding a layer beneath the one you walked in with. Businesses that have outgrown the tools that got them here. People honest about what they don't yet know.

If that sounds like where you are, get in touch.
