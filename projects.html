Creative Engineering

I've spent years building things in software. Now I'm learning how to bring ideas into the physical world, too. Some ideas deserve to leave the screen.

I want to understand how things work, not just what they do, but the individual pieces, materials, mechanisms, and decisions behind them. Then I want to sketch something of my own, figure out how to build it, and see what happens when I bring it to life.

Sometimes that'll mean electronics and code. Sometimes it'll mean gears, 3D printing, painting, or simply making something interesting with my hands.

I'm learning as I go, and I want to share the drawings, experiments, mistakes, and stories along the way.

Part code. Part craftsmanship. All curiosity.

The Process

From an idea to a real thing.

  1. Imagine
  2. → Sketch
  3. → Design
  4. → Build
  5. → Bring to Life
  6. → Tell the Story

Drawing, measuring, designing, printing, soldering, assembling, coding, painting, filming. I'm learning each of these as I go. Not every project needs every step, and not all of them involve software. The point is understanding how something works, and then making something of my own.

What I'm Exploring

Four directions, one curiosity.

Things That Light Up

Light sculptures, interactive desk objects, illuminated miniatures, and the kind of electronics-and-code experiments that are much more fun to look at than to explain.

Things That Move

Robots with a bit of personality, gears, motors, mechanisms, and other ideas that start as a drawing in a notebook and eventually do something in the real world.

Things Worth Making

Studio props, gaming-inspired art, original 3D prints, painted sculptures, and the occasional ridiculous invention. My studio should eventually be full of things I built myself.

The Story Behind It

Notebook sketches, first attempts, the mistakes, what I learned, workshop footage, and eventually the finished videos showing how it all came together.
